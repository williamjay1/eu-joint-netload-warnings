# Simulation amendments and independent review — 8 October 2026

The new simulation programme is an exploratory mechanism analysis developed during the ASMBI revision. It was not preregistered, and its synthetic mechanisms were revised after observing preliminary calculations. It does not create a new untouched empirical test period. The previously inspected European 2024–2025 samples remain reanalysis samples throughout.

The first broad-boundary fixture used weather-state amplitude 0.08 and a positive marginal shift of 0.035. Its initial outputs exposed a reporting bug: `marginal_MAE` was assigned to oracle and wrong-dependence streams although those streams contained no marginal shifts. This affects the first run's reported marginal-error metadata, and that debugging run must not be used as a formal scientific result. The initial broad run also revealed a substantive capacity mechanism: many probabilities close to the gate did not reach the daily top-two rank, so gate proximity alone did not guarantee selected-list changes. Its preliminary near/far comparison must not be described as confirmation of a predicted direction.

The revised primary fixture narrows the intermediate weather-state oscillation to 0.012 and applies a negative regional marginal shift of -0.035. The change creates a more direct gate-boundary stress test; it is a documented exploratory design amendment, not a hypothesis fixed before preliminary runs. The near and far groups are selected with equal counts and interior marginal probabilities, so each has exactly the same average magnitude of marginal perturbation without clipping. Near/far scenarios share the same generated state sequence, event observations and replicate identifier. Oracle and wrong-dependence streams now correctly report zero marginal perturbation. `gate_crossings` is recorded separately from final list changes.

A corrected broad-boundary positive-shift fixture is rerun separately as a control. The directory `wide_boundary_initial` is retained only as debugging/provenance material; it is not a peer scientific comparison. The corrected broad control, rather than the buggy preliminary metadata, should be used if the manuscript discusses rank saturation and directional sensitivity. Shared random seeds support within-replicate pairing; use of the same generator seed across differently parameterised fixtures must not be called independent random-seed replication.

The temporal two-hour fixture has an explicit mode-to-truth mapping: independent occurrence has true probability 0.64 for at least one event, shared occurrence has true probability 0.4. The phase example concerns isolated one-hour hit opportunities with an observed synthetic phase signal versus random phase. It does not use the empirical active-day process definition, does not demonstrate an observed early-process classifier, and cannot establish the existence of a real onset signal.

The main mechanism uses a known shared-uniform spatial mixture with weight w=0.30. Conditional on the weather state, three exceedance indicators share one uniform variable with probability w and use independent uniforms otherwise; the true probability of at least two exceedances is w z+(1-w)(3 z^2-2 z^3). The programmed event generation and this true conditional probability agree. Persistence changes state transitions; the two prevalence configurations change the stationary test distribution. This is not a within-run regime change or a validation of a rolling workload controller. The experiment evaluates prescribed probability perturbations and fixed review policies against known probabilities; it does not train and select a complete forecasting/calibration pipeline on synthetic training data.

Monte Carlo uncertainty is between-replicate variability. `paired_contrasts.csv` reports means of paired contrasts, their standard deviations divided by sqrt(number of repetitions), and normal-approximation Monte Carlo precision intervals. These are simulation-estimation intervals, not empirical European uncertainty intervals or statistical coverage certificates. Near/far and policy contrasts must use matched replicate identifiers. All original and amended simulated output families are labelled synthetic.

The real-data stability calculations using fixed CDF shifts of 0.01 and 0.03 answer a finite perturbation-sensitivity question. They do not cover every calibration-error path or the larger mean cell residuals observed in some weather strata (including approximately 0.145). Cell averages do not identify hourly conditional probability intervals. Therefore the paper may report watchlist robustness inside its stated stress set and lack of attribution precision under empirical cell diagnostics, but cannot certify calibrated probabilities, true marginal-error bounds, safe operating decisions or absence of a dependence contribution.

Independent review has additionally verified the policy module's information contract: mutating all future labels leaves selections and rolling gates identical; modifying later probability vectors leaves previously determined rolling gates unchanged; every archived planned-day count is at most two; mechanism-category totals equal scored selections; and first-early categories equal covered-process counts. Intact process rewards are assigned to onset year/quarter rather than redefined at annual boundaries. Utility inference uses paired calendar-block draws with process rewards on onset day and slot costs on target day, holding forecasts, rules and realised process boundaries fixed. Reported simultaneous bands cover a declared grid for one policy or one named contrast; they do not provide familywise guarantees across all candidate policies, perturbations or research decisions.
