# ASMBI revision design review — 8 October 2026

This review covers the complete pasted revision request, the current ASCE main manuscript and relevant supplement sections, and the existing probability/policy code. It is a design recommendation, not a claim that the additional analyses have run. The requirements matrix contains 72 individually checkable items. Existing figures, fitted streams, inference and examples are historical evidence; every new calculation proposed here is a reanalysis designed after the 2024–2025 original-region and Danish results were inspected.

## Contribution and decision contract

Use the proposed Research Article title, **From Joint Probability Forecasts to Selective Review: Decision Value and Calibration Sensitivity in Electricity Monitoring**, or an equally accurate version. The contribution should answer when joint probability quality translates into selective-review value under capped capacity and temporal clustering, and when calibration changes alter an actual watchlist. It is not a new copula theorem, a new optimal controller, outage prediction or a measured staffing intervention.

The primary decision endpoint is the number/proportion of realised event spells covered in their first six elapsed hours; scenario utility divides by the value of one covered spell. Hourly event hits are a separate secondary endpoint. Every rule faces a daily **capacity ceiling** K=2; it can leave capacity unused except for the explicitly compulsory Top-1 and Top-2 references. A development workload target rho=0.5 selections per forecast-complete day is a separate contract and is not guaranteed by a static gate out of sample. One slot is a nominated delivery-hour window, not an hour of staff labour.

For the basic hourly additive problem, max sum_t a_t(v p_t-c), subject to sum a_t<=K, selecting positive-value probabilities and taking the largest K gives the usual gate c/v. This is elementary decision theory under correctly specified probabilities, constant costs and no duplicate-review externalities. It does not optimise process coverage. Define C(A)=sum_s 1{A intersects E_s}, where E_s is the event-hour subset of [tau_s,tau_s+6 hours). The distinction is essential: the second true hit in the same early window earns no additional C(A), whereas hourly hits remain additive.

## Audited historical facts and interpretation corrections

The main manuscript reports 725 complete scoring days, 17,400 hours, 2,105 q90 event hours and 71 clustered spells. P0 selects 1,450 slots/341 event hours/35 early spells. P1 selects 618/322/32. P2 selects 572 slots/34 early spells. The same deterministic probability stream and tie handling make P1 a subset of P0. Therefore the 832 removed slots contain 19 event hours and 813 non-event hours; their event rate is 19/832=2.28%. Slot use falls by 57.38%. Baseline-hit retention is 322/341=94.43%, whereas event-hour recall is 322/2105=15.30%. Native early coverage falls from 35/71=49.30% to 32/71=45.07%; no allocation claim may hide this loss.

The existing native P2 point estimate uses fewer slots and covers more early spells than P1. Accordingly, a conclusion that P1 is the best policy under an early-spell objective is unsupported even though the existing confidence interval for P2-P1 matched coverage includes zero. P2 has different event-hour trade-offs and substantial yearly variation, both of which must remain visible. The native P1 workload is 618/725=0.8524/day, 70.48% above the 2022 development target 0.5/day, without violating its two-per-day cap.

## Process and information-time reconstruction

The existing `evaluation.event_processes` groups active complete UTC days, permits one complete inactive day between active days, and splits at missing days. Onset is the first true event hour in the first active day, not UTC midnight. `process_masks` correctly uses t<onset+6h, with the lower bound implicit because only the spell's event hours are included. Outcomes are strict net-load exceedances in at least two of three areas. The clustering is a measurement proxy for event persistence, not independently verified meteorological weather systems.

The policy implementation orders a complete 24-hour target day at D-1 12 UTC using the forecast issued for that day. Risk-rise uses preceding six issued probabilities, including earlier targets in the current day's already issued vector. It uses no realised states in those future delivery hours. Candidate ties are resolved by priority, then probability, then ascending target timestamp. Cooldown is a six-hour target separation with absolute time differences; equality at six hours is admissible. A previously selected target near the preceding-day boundary carries forward. Label-incomplete days must not reset the planned-selection state. Replays should start before the scoring interval and carry both probability history and cooldown state into 2024–2025; masking/scoring follows planning.

Keep the original 7-day outcome availability rules and the stricter 15-day Danish fitting convention where used. A new early classifier or price model must use hour ends and explicit availability cutoffs. Calendar and issued forecast trajectories are safe predictors; actual recent delivery-hour states that have not occurred at issue are prohibited. Historical provider revisions are not recoverable by merely reading current files, so all empirical results remain delayed historical replays.

### Forecast-only rolling budget gate

A transparent low-cost controller can choose a threshold from previously issued probabilities by evaluating historical prospective top-K slot counts, avoiding the inaccurate shortcut of using an hourly quantile alone when the daily cap binds. At each issue, take the last 90 forecast-complete target days whose forecasts were already issued before that issue, exclude the newly planned target day, and replay the ordinary top-K gate over those vectors. Select the threshold with largest historical count <=floor(rho*n_days), with a fixed simplicity/tie rule; inherited fixed gate is the warmup fallback if fewer than 30 past complete days exist. Log every issue's gate, calibration-window dates, input issue cutoff and realised planned count. Use no labels for gate updates, including during years whose labels are eventually unavailable for scoring.

Window 90 and target rho=0.5 are protocol choices made now and are not preregistered. Optional workload-contract grid rho in {0.25,0.5,1.0,1.5}, daily cap fixed2, may produce descriptive deployment curves. Freeze the grid before computation and do not pick the most favourable test point as the main result. A rolling target based on past distributions is not an exact annual quota or a guaranteed online budget controller. If an exact causal token budget is additionally implemented, label it as a different resource contract and make both its warm-start credit and its daily ceiling explicit.

## Mechanism categories and lost-process audit

Assign selected slots in chronological target order to mutually exclusive categories: non-event; first selected early event in a process; additional selected event in a process whose early stage was already covered; event slots in a process that has no early selected event and which occur after its early window. For full accounting, late selections before a later early selection cannot occur because early windows precede later targets; nevertheless define the rule in code and verify counts sum exactly to selected slots. If the chosen taxonomy instead uses an additional category for repeated late hits in an entirely late-only process, state it and retain all categories. Do not label every first event-hour hit as early.

Compare categories for P0, P1, rolling gate and the common-threshold cooldown/rise variants. Decompose the removed P0\P1 set with the same labels. Audit all three early spells lost by P1 relative to P0, retaining spell onset/duration/event hours/exceedance magnitude/forecast probabilities/weather cell, and whether lost early coverage was due to a low gate-crossing probability or rank. This is a complete failure-case table; it is not an independent validation cohort.

## Utility curves and inference

For early-process value, U_s/v_s=C(A)-r_s N(A), r_s=c/v_s. Include all policies and no review, whose utility is zero. Use a declared cost grid covering zero, the relevant crossover range and the high-cost region where no review dominates. P1-P0 equals -3+832 r_s, with point-estimate crossover 3/832=0.003606. P2-P1 equals 2+46r_s on the historical native counts, so P2 pointwise dominates P1 for this narrowly defined early objective and nonnegative slot costs; this does not imply dominance for hourly event hits or all refitted specifications. Acknowledge this algebra before presenting any recommendation. Use secondary U_h/v_h=TP-r_h N as a separate curve, not an undocumented composite utility.

Use synchronous calendar block draws across policies, model streams, cost grid and curves. Preserve intact realised spell contributions attributed to onset day as in the existing implementation, while slot costs are attributed to actual target day. Jointly sample the full daily contribution vector so utility uncertainty respects the pairing between rewards and selections. Costs and process rewards can occur on different days; the inference approximation must be disclosed, and 7/28-day sensitivity should complement primary14-day blocks. Missing calendar dates remain in the lattice. Do not reconnect new spells at bootstrap seams. A confidence band can use the bootstrap maximum deviation over the fixed grid; otherwise display pointwise intervals and do not claim simultaneous superiority after selecting curve extrema. Fixed-flow intervals condition on fitted models, chosen rules and realised process boundaries.

Keep exact hypergeometric retained-coverage calculations as a separate exposure-standardisation analysis. Use explicit B labels; historical min(P1,P2) is an observed common exposure and changes when a new rule is included, so it is not an external operational budget. Native strategies under equal ex ante resource/cost contracts are the primary deployment comparison.

## Calibration perturbations and actual decision stability

State a finite, transparent perturbation family before running. Existing six-cell mean-residual offsets can supply an empirical scale, but they do not give a pointwise conditional probability confidence interval. An efficient main design uses common signed offsets of region threshold CDFs with magnitudes specified by archived residual scale and a fixed illustrative grid; clipping to (1e-12,1-1e-12) and each clipping amount are logged. Include joint sign combinations and the original zero perturbation, recompute Gaussian/t event probabilities with identical R, and replay each rule from the beginning of its planning context.

With fixed copula and u_j=F_j(q_j), use |p(u)-p(u')|<=sum_j|u_j-u'_j| as an elementary coupling/union-bound explanation. Define epsilon_t from the stated family, not as a certified sampling interval. Gate status cannot change when |p_t-gate|>epsilon_t; ranking requires enough separation from competing candidates as well. In a Top-K cap, crossing the gate alone does not imply selection. A sufficient exact-ranking argument compares candidate probability intervals with every competing candidate at the selection boundary; for rise scores use propagated score intervals or empirical full replays. With cooldown, even a locally stable decision can be changed by an earlier perturbation-induced selection, so only a full path replay supports a whole-list stability claim.

Report observed stable selections, stable rejections and critical hours across the specified family; list overlap/Jaccard, switched targets, membership flips conditional on gate versus rank, native hourly hits and early coverage ranges. Attribute early spell differences to the first changed responsible selection without treating the decomposition as causal evidence. Plot the critical probability region, marginal conditional threshold positions and Gaussian/t differences near the gate. Titles and captions should say **sensitivity under specified perturbations**, not uncertainty certification.

## Numerical assurance

The existing elliptical integrator computes pair/triple CDFs along an SPD correlation path, handles the common radial scale of finite-t at R=I, and uses doubled Gauss-Legendre orders plus an adaptive integral for the baseline. Its reported discrepancy is a convergence proxy, not a certified absolute bound. Reproducing the archived Gaussian stream to 2e-12 proves deterministic reproduction only.

Recompute a stratified audit set spanning probability bins, weather cells, conditional thresholds, low eigenvalues of R, Gaussian/t families and gate-near rows at the production tolerance and successively stricter tolerances/order ceilings. Include all rows that could change a selection at the numerical discrepancy scale and all affected early-process candidate hours. Compare with an independent high-precision multivariate CDF implementation using independent randomised scrambles/seeds and a reported reference uncertainty. Where feasible, audit the full score domain at tighter tolerance to compare the resulting paired Brier contrast and complete selections. A useful practical acceptance target is that the change in the 1.9e-5 contrast is at most5percent of its magnitude and does not change any main policy ordering; report actual achieved precision and do not silently convert this target to a mathematical guarantee. Log negative count corrections and renormalisation, because cancellation in pair/triple combinations can matter near boundaries.

## Early-window classifier and limited refitting

Train one regularised logistic model first, using issued p_t, forecast differences/preceding issued-risk maximum, hour/season calendar, forecast-only weather summaries and optional daily trajectory features known at the same issue. The target is E_t times an indicator of being within the realised parent process's first6h. Retain all eligible mature training labels with a fixed class weighting policy; no test-driven balancing or new algorithm suite. Fit/scaling/hyperparameter selection are chronological in past-only windows. Use coarse fixed regularisation candidates on development data, freeze final specification, and evaluate 2024–2025 as reanalysis. Repeat with a few rolling origins to assess whether training-period changes alter results; the folds are not independent replications.

A label belongs in the fitting set only when its observed hour end meets the original region-specific availability lag and the requisite process-boundary context has matured. Process windows crossing a fold boundary require sufficient previous context and exclusion/censoring where uncertain. Since fewer than 100 original spells support inference, report positive hours AND independent spell support per fold, failed/no-positive fits, Brier/reliability of early probabilities and utility, with no unauthorised future realised event features. The inherited quarterly margin/dependence refits already vary training history; if complete raw-pipeline refitting cannot be performed under available resources, label the new rolling exercise as policy/early-model refit on fixed archived forecast streams and do not claim full-pipeline estimation uncertainty.

## Mechanism simulation protocol

Use ADEMP: aims, data-generating mechanisms, estimands, methods and performance. Do not seek a favourable headline percentage. Minimum mechanisms are (i) errors of comparable aggregate size concentrated near versus far from the gate, (ii) independent versus short/long persistent event states at matched mean prevalence, (iii) a known Gaussian or finite-t dependence family with thresholds in middle versus conditional upper tail, (iv) event/probability distribution shifts driving workload drift, and (v) onset signal present versus absent. A two-state Markov/weather-duration generator is sufficient to expose temporal repetition, with the transition probabilities, true event probabilities, conditional signal and observation-error distributions written explicitly.

Separate training/development/evaluation draws and random seeds, retaining common random numbers for paired policy comparisons. State which probabilities are oracle truth and which are estimated/miscalibrated. Present hourly probability loss, native workload, early-process coverage, utility and list stability by scenario. For rates, report sqrt(phat*(1-phat)/R); for paired mean contrasts report SD of replicate contrasts/sqrt(R). The existing36/100 ineffective compatibility acceptances have MCSEapproximately0.048 and should not be described as a precise population error rate. Zero successes in100 repetitions is not a guarantee of zero error. Choose repetitions by MC precision and cap runtime only after a pilot. Synthetic mechanisms are explicitly synthetic and do not substitute for prospective European validation.

## Danish and price sensitivity endpoints

DK1/DK2 combinations share924 German-French event hours, representing56.3% and50.6% of their events. They are correlated transfer settings, not two independent external replications. Define the Danish-dependent hourly mask as the joint event with Danish exceedance that would disappear if Denmark were excluded, consistent with the existing717/902-hour subset.

For the primary nested early endpoint, retain each parent combination's realised spell onset and6h window; count only parent spells having at least one Danish-dependent event in that early window and cover a spell only if a selected Danish-dependent target lies there. This answers whether early success reaches the added region without relabelling a late Danish event as an early parent success. Provide a companion clearly secondary analysis recomputing subset-only spells/onsets, because it asks onset of Danish-dependent events instead. For scenario utility include all review costs for the parent's watchlist when measuring Danish-specific reward; a post hoc subset of slots cannot be called an executable regional-only rule. Report subset opportunity count, covered count, null-opportunity parent spells, year breakdown and uncertainty.

Price sensitivity uses only existing Danish day-ahead prices and must state that archived historical release vintages were not recovered. The issue clock follows the day-ahead auction, but a current final price is not authenticated as historically available. Compare price ranking under dailyTopK, a development price+calendar logistic model, probability-only and probability+price under identical caps and evaluation domains. Fit prices/calendars only on mature development labels; target-hour price predictors enter under **assumed issue-time availability**, so any increment is a sensitivity result rather than validated real-time incremental operational value. No price-response or warning-caused effect is inferable.

## Writing, figures, release and stopping rule

The main manuscript must explain the random object and decision objective independently; detailed provider accounting and fit parameters belong in Supporting Information without hiding conclusion-critical assumptions. Remove the ASCE Practical Applications section and ASCE-specific upload ban when packaging for ASMBI, subject to verification of the current official ASMBI guide. Target abstract <=250 words, <=5 keywords, editable source/manuscript, data accessibility statement and accompanying supporting files. Do not claim updated analyses are inside an existing DOI until the author archives a new release. Preserve all current F raw data/archival submission files and both historical DOI records.

Main figures should prioritise mechanism decomposition, native executable workload/earlycoverage, cost utility, perturbation/list stability and mechanism simulations. Move quiet-day and repetitive robustness plots to supporting information. Vector output with readable labels and genuine high-resolution bitmap alternatives follows the user's requirements; no upscaled fake900dpi detail. Public reproducibility should allow rebuilding the main tables from derived hourly probabilities/issue timestamps/selection markers/mature labels/process IDs/exclusion reasons if provider terms permit, with a distinct raw-provider retrieval and training layer. A repository of final summaries alone is insufficient for the new claim.

At completion, classify every matrix row as implemented+verified, implemented unverified, retained existing evidence, or explicitly unresolved with its effect on the manuscript. If a simple budget gate explains all allocation benefit and marginal perturbations hardly alter decisions, frame the contribution as a rigorous applied evaluation and identified failure boundaries. Do not advertise a new control method or an empirically optimal gate. A strong null result requires sufficiently precise evidence about the proposed decision increment, not merely a nonsignificant p-value.

Resource expectation: most policy crosses and cost/selection diagnostics run on cached hourly predictions and should need CPU time and small output tables; the substantive expensive parts are independent numerical accuracy checks, new early/price refits and mechanism simulation. No GPU-heavy architecture or new raw download is required by this protocol. Perform a small stratified pilot before scaling numerical/simulation work, record runtime and storage, and do not fabricate run-time estimates before seeing the pilot. All new outputs belong in the ASMBI D workspace.
