# Final scientific figure captions

Figure 1. Evaluation workflow for cross-border joint warnings. (a) Observations and archived forecasts define area-specific seasonal monitoring events. (b) The controlled 2×2 comparison changes the marginal map and dependence family while holding event thresholds, samples, weather and correlation matrices fixed. The main review comparison feeds M0 probabilities to three forecast-only rules; a secondary comparison applies fixed P1 to all four forecast streams. (c) Marginal sensitivity, joint probability loss and review performance are assessed separately. The study uses delayed historical replay; Danish combinations test transfer of the fixed specifications.

Figure 2. Information and operating timeline. (a) GEFS initialisation is D−1 00 UTC; the replay issues all target-day risks at D−1 12 UTC, giving 12–35-hour forecast-to-target leads. Noon local day-ahead gate closure is D−1 11 UTC in winter and 10 UTC in summer, before the issue. The 12-hour weather allowance is a replay convention rather than verified historical arrival. (b) Selected operating hours can prompt checks of supply availability, reserves, import headroom and transmission constraints, supporting later-day-ahead review and intraday preparation. These checks are proposed uses, not interventions evaluated in the study.

Figure 3. Workload and early coverage for three fixed review rules. Rows align policies within each area combination. (a) Filled circles show native selected-slot counts. (b) Filled circles show native q90 first-six-hour process coverage, and hollow triangles show exact expected coverage after uniform, label-blind retention to the indicated common total budget: 572 slots for DE–LU/FR/BE, 432 for DE–LU/FR/DK1 and 492 for DE–LU/FR/DK2. Small vertical offsets within each policy row distinguish the symbols and carry no quantitative meaning. The evaluation covers 2024–2025 with one inactive day allowed between active days. P0 forces two reviews per complete day; P1 applies the probability gate and permits zero; P2 combines risk-rise priority, a gate and cooldown. The three fixed policies are not an optimised performance frontier.

Figure 4. Annual native coverage and workload for compulsory and gated review. (a) Panels report q90 first-six-hour process coverage for P0 and P1; annotations give covered spells divided by spells whose onset falls in the year. (b) Panels report selected review slots per complete scoring day. Spell boundaries remain intact across years. These native comparisons show the observed allocation trade-off and are not matched-workload contrasts.

Figure 5. Gate and dependence contrasts in the original and Danish combinations. (a) P1 minus P0 first-six-hour coverage at the same total selected-slot exposure; positive values favour the probability gate. (b) M3 minus M1 Brier loss under the additionally mapped margins and shared correlation matrices; negative values favour selected t dependence. Points and bars are q90 estimates and existing paired 95% intervals using 14-day calendar blocks. Review intervals resample intact spells by onset day and condition on fixed policies, retention fractions and spell boundaries. Score intervals synchronously resample common calendar data. Intervals are pointwise, not multiplicity-adjusted. Danish local-weather specifications are primary; the original-region analysis is reanalysis.

Figure 6. A forecast-only quiet-day example. The case is the first complete target day in 2022–2023 for which all 24 issued M0 (original dynamic Gaussian) q90 joint-event probabilities were below the fixed P1 gate of 0.25. Case selection used forecasts only. (a) The full probability range shows issued risks relative to the 0.25 gate. (b) An explicitly labelled low-probability detail shows the same risks and highlights the two P0 selections. (c) P0 selects the two highest probabilities despite their low level; P1 allocates no slots. Realised-event labels are checked only after selecting the date and do not trigger allocation. The example illustrates the rule, rather than estimating its average value.

Figure S1. Post hoc examples of gains and losses from risk-rise priority. The same two previously selected cases illustrate directional differences between P1 and P2. Upper panels show issued joint risk; lower lanes separate forecast-only planned hours and realised event hours. The tan bands mark the first six elapsed hours after observed onset. The grey P2 band in the second case marks the cooldown after a planned hour at −1 h. Time is relative to 00 UTC on the date shown. Observed onset and events annotate the cases and do not trigger allocation. These illustrations are not independent predictive validation.
